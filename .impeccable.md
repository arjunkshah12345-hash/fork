## Design Context

### Users

FORK is for engineers and technical leads who need to compare several real implementations of the same coding task without mixing candidate changes into their checkout. They use it during focused development and review work to launch a run, monitor progress, compare evidence, and ship one winning branch with confidence.

### Brand Personality

Restrained, precise, decisive. The product should feel calm while work is active and conclusive when evaluation finishes. Language is concise and operational: it reports real state, names failures plainly, and always gives a recovery path.

### Aesthetic Direction

Dark graphite product workspace with quiet steel and ivory signals, informed by Linear and Vercel restraint. Geist Sans carries interface copy and Geist Mono carries status, commands, scores, files, and time. Prefer smaller type, divisions, and whitespace to containers. A sparse dither field is reserved for live execution texture. Avoid card mosaics, fake analytics, gradients, neon, glass effects, glows, emoji, and decorative charts.

### Design Principles

1. Keep one dominant action per view: compose a run, inspect a run, or publish its winner.
2. Show all three strategies together and make every status legible without relying on color alone.
3. Start with concise evidence; disclose logs, diffs, checks, and findings on demand.
4. Use motion only to explain entry or state change, and fully respect reduced-motion preferences.
5. Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, stable layouts, tabular numerals, and honest empty/error states.
